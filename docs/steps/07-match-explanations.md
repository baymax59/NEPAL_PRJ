# Step 7: Why a case matched (explanations)

Status: **done and verified** (2026-11-04). 158 tests pass. Checked on real judgments.

## 1. What this module does

- **Detects the statutory provisions** each judgment cites ("Section 302 IPC", "Article 21 of the Constitution", "Order VII Rule 11 CPC") and stores them with mention counts and pages.
- **Adds `reasons` to every search result card.** These are short, checkable statements:
  - "Cites Section 302 IPC, as in your search"
  - "Contains 5 of 6 search words: retired, government, denied, pension, arrears"
  - "Passage on p.1 is close in meaning to your search (similarity 0.77)"
  - "Words not found: servant"
  - "No citation detected of: Section 438 CrPC"
- **`POST /api/cases/{display_id}/explain`** gives the full breakdown for the details page ("AI similarity analysis", section E of the spec):
  - shared and missing words (with how many passages contain each);
  - shared provisions (with pages), and provisions only in the search;
  - what the case cites most;
  - the 5 closest passages, each saying whether it matched by wording, by meaning or both;
  - differences;
  - a method note on what the explanation does and doesn't mean.
- **The case details** (`GET /api/cases/{id}`) now include `provisions`.

## 2. Why it is needed

A relevance score alone gives no reason to trust a result. Reviewers need to see **why** a case was returned and **what doesn't match**, and be able to check both in the source.

Everything here is computed from the stored text, and nothing is generated by an AI model. Every point refers to words, provisions or pages that can be checked.

## 6. The code, explained

### Detecting provisions (`explain/provisions.py`)

Citation styles found in the real judgments, and how each is handled:

| Style | Example | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Section + "of the" + Act | `Section 18 of the SARFAESI Act` | Section 18 of the SARFAESI Act |
| Section + abbreviation | `Section 482 of the Cr.P.C.`, `u/s.173 CrPC` | Section 482 CrPC, Section 173 CrPC |
| "read with" chains | `Sections 395, 364 and 302 read with Section 34 of the IPC` | all four → IPC |
| Headnote, Act first | `Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 – ss.96, 105 & Or.IX, r.13` | Sections 96, 105 CPC; Order IX Rule 13 CPC |
| Headnote, abbreviation | `IPC – ss. 395, 302 and 302 r/w s.34.` | Sections 395, 302, 34 IPC |
| Articles | `Articles 14 and 21` | Articles 14, 21 of the Constitution (not if "of the Convention…") |
| CPC orders | `Order VII Rule 11` | Order VII Rule 11 CPC |
| **No Act named** | `u/s.96`, `Section 4 of the Act`, `Section 12 of the said Act` | **skipped** |

- **Precision over recall.** A section is only recorded when its Act is named at that point. A wrong "cites Section 302 IPC" would mislead a reviewer.
- **One name per Act.** "Cr.P.C.", "CrPC" and "Code of Criminal Procedure" → **CrPC**. "DSPE Act" and "Delhi Special Police Establishment Act" → **DSPE Act**. The same applies to IPC, CPC, PMLA, NDPS, SARFAESI, SEBI, PC Act, Land Acquisition Act, and the 2023 codes BNS, BNSS and BSA. "IT Act" is left alone, because it can mean the Income Tax Act or the Information Technology Act.
- **Section level.** `Section 167(2)` and `Section 167` count as the same provision.
- **Known limitations:**
  - The Act's year is not used, so "CrPC 1898" (the old Code) is grouped with today's CrPC.
  - Bare `s.N` references (common inside headnotes) are not attributed to an Act.

**Accuracy checks on the real text:**
- **Random sample:** 17 of 17 sampled mentions were labelled correctly.
- **Act-name list:** reviewing the Act names found turned up three problems, all fixed with tests:
  - a bogus "B of the Act" (from "Section 9 B of the Act");
  - a judge's initial glued to an Act name ("J. Securities and Exchange Board of India Act");
  - several Acts appearing under more than one name.
- **A false statement in a real explanation:** *Boby v. State of Kerala*, a murder appeal, was explained as "Does not cite: Section 302 IPC". Its text uses the "read with" and "IPC – ss." styles above, which weren't handled yet. After the fix: **Section 302 IPC on pages 1, 2, 6, 7, 20**, and the number of cases citing Section 302 IPC went from 10 to 11.

### Wording absences honestly

Saying something is **absent** is the riskiest kind of claim, so absences are worded as exactly what was checked:
- **"Words not found: eyewitness"**: words are matched literally (by stem). The case might still say "eye-witness".
- **"No citation detected of: Section 438 CrPC"**: detection can miss unusual citation styles.

Every explanation also carries a `method_note`. It says the explanation shows why the search returned the case, not that the cases are legally the same, and that provisions are detected automatically.

### Card reasons and the full explanation (`explain/service.py`)

- **Search words:** the query is turned into its meaningful words (common words like "the" and excluded `-words` are dropped) and their stems. For each result, one SQL query counts how many passages contain each stem.
- **Provisions:** the query's provisions are compared with the provisions stored for each case.
- **Closest passage:** taken from the semantic search (page and similarity).
- **The full explanation** additionally fetches the case's 3 closest passages by meaning (above the 0.60 cut-off) and 3 best by wording, interleaved into up to 5. Each says how it matched.
- **Access is checked first.** Explaining a restricted or missing case returns the same 404 as the details page.
- **Speed:** search with reasons takes ~90–140 ms; an explanation takes ~60 ms.

## 7. How it connects to other modules

- Uses Step 4's page text, Step 5's keyword index and Step 6's embeddings.
- **Step 8:** when the query is a whole uploaded case, the same explanation adds case-vs-case differences: outcome, decision year, and provisions cited by only one side.
- **Step 11:** result cards show `reasons`. The details page shows the explanation (section E) and `provisions`. The `method_note` must be shown with it.
- **Step 12:** entity extraction builds on this. Provisions are done; parties, courts and dates come next.
- **Step 13:** the chatbot can cite provisions with their pages.

## 10. Common errors and fixes

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| `relation "document_provisions" does not exist` | Migration 0003 not applied | `uv run alembic upgrade head` |
| Case details show `"provisions": []` for every case | Provisions not built for existing documents | `uv run python -m scripts.extract_provisions` |
| A provision you can see in the judgment is "not detected" | A citation style the rules don't cover | Copy the exact sentence into a new test in `test_provisions.py`, extend the patterns, run `scripts.extract_provisions` |
| A wrong provision appears | A pattern is too loose | Same: add the sentence as a test that expects `[]`, then tighten the pattern |
| The same Act appears under two names | Alias missing | Add it to `KNOWN_ACTS` in `provisions.py`, then rerun `scripts.extract_provisions` |
| A reason says a word is "not found" though a variant is in the text | Matching is literal (by stem): "eyewitness" ≠ "eye-witness" | Expected and worded honestly; the meaning-based passages often still find it |
| `�` characters in a terminal printout | Windows console display of non-ASCII (– … ’) | Not a data problem. The API returns proper UTF-8 |
