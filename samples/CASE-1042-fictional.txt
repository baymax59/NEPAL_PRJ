FICTIONAL SAMPLE CASE - FOR TESTING ONLY. NOT A REAL CASE OR REAL PERSONS.
Case reference: CASE-1042 (illustrative)

Summary of complaint

The complainant, a fictional wholesale supplier referred to here as Supplier A, states that between
March and August of the year in question it supplied textile goods worth a large sum to a retail
firm run by the accused, referred to as Person B, on credit. Part payment was made at first.
Later cheques issued by the accused were dishonoured and the balance remained unpaid.

The complainant alleges that the accused never intended to pay, induced the supplies by
dishonest promises, and later sold the goods and kept the money. On this basis a First
Information Report was registered for the offences of cheating, criminal breach of trust and
criminal conspiracy under Sections 406, 420 and 120B of the Indian Penal Code.

Stand of the accused

The accused says that the parties had a long-running business relationship, that the dispute
concerns the balance of an account and the quality of some consignments, and that a civil suit
for recovery of money is already pending between them. According to the accused, a purely
commercial dispute has been given the colour of a criminal offence to put pressure on him to
pay. He has applied to the High Court under Section 482 of the Code of Criminal Procedure to
quash the First Information Report and the criminal proceedings.

Points for consideration

1. Whether the allegations, taken at face value, show dishonest intention at the beginning of the
   transaction, which is needed for the offence of cheating.
2. Whether a breach of contract or failure to pay for goods, without more, can amount to criminal
   breach of trust or cheating.
3. Whether the criminal proceedings are an abuse of the process of law that should be quashed.

Current status: investigation pending; no charge-sheet filed. (Fictional.)
